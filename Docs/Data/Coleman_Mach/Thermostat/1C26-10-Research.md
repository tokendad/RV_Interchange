## 3. Historical identity and company lineage

### Exact product identity

A used-parts listing contains four photographs of the exact thermostat. The rear label reads:

- `TYPE 1C26-10`
- `153-6616`

The front is branded **Coleman** and marked **REMOTE CONTROL**. It has a mechanical temperature scale from 50 to 90 °F, a heat/off/cool system selector, and a low/high/fan selector. A rear photograph shows six attached pigtail conductors.

The strongest exact evidence is therefore the photographed label itself, not a modern catalog description.

### Brand and company lineage

Coleman-Mach’s official history states that the Coleman Company introduced its first RV air conditioner, the PolarPal, in 1967. The RV climate-control line later operated as **Coleman-Mach under Airxcel**.

Official legacy manuals from 2001–2004 identify the manufacturer as:

> RV Products  
> A Division of Airxcel, Inc.  
> Wichita, Kansas

The exact `1C26-10` thermostat carries the older standalone Coleman script rather than the later RV Products or Coleman-Mach presentation. That suggests an earlier branding generation, but no reliable production date, first-use date, or exact corporate transition date for this thermostat was located.

### Unresolved original manufacturer issue

The model prefix `1C26` resembles White-Rodgers thermostat nomenclature, and PDX RV links a White-Rodgers `1C26` manual from a page titled for Coleman `1C26-10`. However:

- the exact photographed thermostat is Coleman-branded;
- it has six factory pigtails;
- it has two-speed fan control;
- it is arranged as an RV remote control;
- the linked White-Rodgers thermostat has a screw-terminal residential subbase, one fan output, and a 24 VAC rating.

No primary evidence was found proving that White-Rodgers manufactured the Coleman `1C26-10`. That possibility remains **unresolved** and should not be inferred merely from the shared `1C26` string.

### Electrical architecture

The exact thermostat’s combination of:

- heat/cool selection;
- high- and low-speed fan control;
- six attached leads; and
- installation in RV furnace/air-conditioner systems

strongly matches the later Coleman/RV Products **12 VDC relay-control** architecture.

The later official 7330 heat/cool thermostat uses these six functions:

| Function | Typical later Coleman designation | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Positive control supply | `R` | +12 VDC |
| Negative/common | `B` | −12 VDC/common |
| Compressor | `Y` | Energizes compressor relay |
| High fan | `GH` | Energizes high-speed fan relay |
| Low fan | `GL` | Energizes low-speed fan relay |
| Furnace/heat | `W` | Heat or furnace control |

This table is **not an exact 1C26-10 wiring diagram**. It is a family-level functional comparison.

One owner recorded these labels before removing an old `1C26-10`:

- brown → `GL`;
- green → `GH`;
- large white → `B`;
- large black → `Y`;
- small white → `W`;
- small red → `R`.

That mapping is anecdotal and differs from colors visible on another photographed unit. It proves why **wire function must be traced and labeled rather than inferred by color**.

### Exact voltage conclusion

No exact label or factory sheet giving the `1C26-10` voltage was found. The most defensible conclusion is:

> The Coleman `1C26-10` was probably intended to switch a 12 VDC RV thermostat/control-box circuit, while the rooftop air conditioner itself used 115/120 VAC.

This is a **strong inference**, not a directly verified nameplate rating.

### Installation-document discrepancy

PDX RV’s page is titled **“Coleman Thermostat 1c26-10 Installation,”** but its linked PDF is:

- White-Rodgers installation instructions for generic models `1C20` and `1C26`;
- White-Rodgers part `37-6335B`;
- a residential Class II thermostat rated **24 VAC**;
- intended for four- or five-wire systems;
- equipped with a single `G` fan terminal and AUTO/ON fan operation;
- mounted on a screw-terminal switching subbase.

The exact Coleman `1C26-10` instead has:

- six factory pigtails;
- separate high- and low-fan circuits;
- Coleman RV “remote control” styling;
- no matching residential terminal subbase.

**Conclusion:** the PDX PDF is a near-match document that appears to have been associated by model-name similarity. It must not be used as the wiring manual for the exact Coleman part.

### Safety and installation caution

Do not energize or jumper the six thermostat wires until the rooftop/control-box model and each conductor’s function are identified. A residential 24 VAC thermostat, an RV 12 VDC thermostat, and a 24 VAC park-model Coleman control box can share familiar letter designations while using different power arrangements.

## 8. Final evidence summary

### Verified facts

- Coleman `1C26-10` is a real RV wall thermostat.
- The exact photographed unit also carries `153-6616`.
- It is a mechanical heat/off/cool thermostat with a 50–90 °F scale, low/high fan control, and six attached leads.
- Only used or legacy listings were found; no current factory product page for the exact number was located.
- No exact Coleman factory wiring or installation manual was found.

### Strong inference

The unit belongs to an older Coleman RV remote-control system that switched rooftop air-conditioner and furnace relays through low-voltage wiring, probably 12 VDC. Later official Coleman thermostats use the same six control functions: `R`, `B`, `Y`, `GH`, `GL`, and `W`.

### Replacement conclusion

`7330G3351` is the strongest successor lead because:

1. owners and technicians independently proposed it for `1C26-10`;
2. one owner reported that the manufacturer recommended it;
3. it is an official Coleman heat/cool analog thermostat;
4. its six control functions closely match the old unit.

Nevertheless, the cross-reference was not found in a Coleman parts catalog, service bulletin, or installation guide. Therefore it should be treated as a **candidate requiring system verification**, not a guaranteed plug-in replacement.

`8330-3362` and `9420-351` are later downstream candidates, but the evidence does not support skipping directly from `1C26-10` to either model without confirming the control box and wiring.

### Critical exclusion

Do **not** use White-Rodgers `1C26`, `1C26-101`, or the PDX-linked 24 VAC manual as an exact replacement/wiring reference. The shared number is misleading.

### Information needed for a definitive replacement

A definitive interchange requires photographs or numbers from:

- the rooftop air-conditioner model/data plate;
- the ceiling assembly or relay/control-box label;
- every old thermostat wire with its original terminal or circuit label;
- measured control voltage and polarity;
- whether the furnace is controlled by the same thermostat;
- whether “FAN” means continuous fan while LOW/HIGH choose speed.
